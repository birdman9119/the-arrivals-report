Methodology

Where the numbers come from, and what they mean.

Source

All data is the U.S. Department of Transportation's Bureau of Transportation Statistics On-Time Performance database, which covers scheduled passenger flights by U.S. reporting carriers on domestic routes. Each page shows a rolling window of the most recent months available — currently {{ month_count }} months, the latest being {{ month }}.

Definitions

On-time means the flight arrived within 15 minutes of its scheduled arrival time — the DOT's standard definition. Cancelled and diverted flights are counted separately, not as on-time.

Year to date is the headline number on every route: the running total of all flights from January through the latest available month, weighted by flight volume. The smaller "Rolling window" line shows the most recent {{ month_count }} months of history.

Avg. delay is the mean arrival delay in minutes across all flights by that airline on that route (early arrivals count as zero, matching the DOT's "ArrDelayMinutes" measure).

Trend is the airline's on-time percentage for each of the last {{ month_count }} months.

Morning vs. evening compares scheduled departures before noon with departures after 5pm on the same route.

What this is not

There is no public data on baggage claim wait times by airline — no airport or agency publishes it. This site intentionally doesn't pretend otherwise.

Update schedule

Data is refreshed monthly, within days of each DOT release.
